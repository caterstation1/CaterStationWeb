# CaterStation research notes

Researched public CaterStation pages on 13 July 2026: homepage, all-products collection, corporate catering navigation entry, delivery page, dietary/FAQ content and representative product pages including 100–120 person Breakfast Pack, Mid-Year Christmas Slider Kit 20–25ppl, Bowl Bundle 40–50ppl and Mini Dipper Donut Kit.

## 1. Existing visual identity
- The identity is bold and ecommerce-driven: pink brand accents, cardboard-brown packaging cues, dense product photography and blunt promotional messaging such as “Bring flavour to your function.”
- The strongest distinctive cue is the station-in-a-box idea: food arrives in kits, is opened and set up, then everyone builds their own plate.

## 2. Colours, typography, spacing and image style
- Brand signals include CaterStation pink, black text, warm cardboard browns and bright food colours.
- Typography appears bold but the Shopify product grid reduces many names/prices to repetitive small ecommerce strings.
- Photography is colourful and product-led, with several product pages showing many setup/detail images.

## 3. Current page structure
- Homepage: city modal, menu/header, promo ticker, hero, featured product rows, mission line, repeated trust ticker, bowl series, blog cards and FAQs.
- Collection: category chips for Shop All, 10–15, 20–25, Bundles, Party Packs, Party Packs 100+ and Add-Ons, then a long product grid.
- Product pages: gallery, title, price, options, add to cart, description, add-ons, footer.

## 4. Current navigation and city-selection system
- A city chooser asks “Where are you shopping?” with Auckland, Wellington and “I’ll pick later.”
- Header shows City with Auckland/Wellington dropdown plus primary links: Home, Shop, Delivery, Contact, Dusty Hots, Dietaries, FAQs and Events.
- Delivery is split by Auckland and Wellington in navigation.

## 5. Product discovery process
- Discovery is mostly collection/category based. Helpful group-size categories exist, but the grid begins with large party packs and product names are long.
- Product pages contain useful specifics: serveware options, proteins, dietary labels, add-ons and setup notes.

## 6. Strongest existing elements
- Clear “Get Quote” utility.
- Strong FAQ answers for hot holding, people counts, invoice payment, delivery zones and dietaries.
- Memorable promises: “Delivered Hot,” “No Stress,” “Dietaries covered,” “Ready to serve.”
- Large-event packages already exist for 40–50, 60–75, 75–95, 100+ and 150 person occasions.

## 7. Sources of friction or confusion
- Busy corporate buyers must infer the best item from a long collection grid rather than being guided by occasion, headcount and date.
- The homepage repeats some FAQ content and trust tickers, making key conversion information feel buried.
- Product naming is operational rather than shopper-first; examples include very long “Dietaries covered DF, Gluten friendly, Vegan” titles.
- “Order online” vs “Get quote” is present but not explained by threshold or use case.

## 8. Missing trust information
- Limited above-the-fold proof for corporate buyers: no visible office-client logos, review snippets, delivery success stats or “invoice-friendly” cue near the hero.
- Dietary caveats are honest and useful but need a calmer presentation so confidence is not lost.

## 9. Missing conversion information
- Headcount guidance should appear before or beside product cards.
- Delivery fees, lead time, delivery windows, hot-holding time and invoice payment should be compressed into a buyer-confidence strip.
- Large-event quote threshold should be explicit: e.g. “50+ unsure? 100+ recommended quote.”

## 10. Mobile usability issues
- Navigation has many items and nested event links; mobile buyers need a shorter task-first menu.
- Long product titles and repetitive price text make scanning difficult.
- Sticky order/quote actions would reduce scroll fatigue.

## 11. Content worth retaining
- “Bring the team together,” “It’s coming in hot,” delivered hot, build-your-own stations, dietaries covered, invoice payment, 48-hour lead time, next-day by 3pm where possible, 15/30-minute delivery windows and hot-holding guidance.

## 12. Content to shorten or reposition
- Repeated FAQ blocks should be consolidated.
- SEO footer paragraphs can move lower.
- Blog cards are useful but should not compete with the primary office-catering conversion path.
